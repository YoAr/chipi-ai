The AI does the legwork. You make the decisions.

Chipi uses AI to read, search, compare and draft, so you don't have to start from a blank page. Here is what it does, what it never does, and where it can be wrong.

What it never does

  • Send anything in your name without your OK. Every direct email waits for you to approve it. This is enforced in our code, not just promised.
  • Apply to jobs on its own. Auto-apply is off until you switch it on, and then only on platforms you choose that allow it.
  • Decide about you. Scores and suggestions are advice. Nothing with a legal or similarly significant effect is decided by AI alone.
  • Judge you. A pitch with no reply is information about the pitch, not a verdict on you.
  • Invent facts. Drafts may only state what's in your CV or what you told us. A rate is shown only with its source.

How we check its work

Before a draft reaches you, a second, separate AI check reviews it with a fresh view. It confirms that:

  • every claim about you can be traced to your CV or your answers;
  • any rate matches your rate range and is never below the minimum you set;
  • the message is short and has one clear ask;
  • the channel is allowed (no automated messages where a platform forbids them).

A draft that fails a check is fixed or held back, and you can see which checks passed. Rules that must never be broken, such as approval before sending and your minimum rate, are enforced by ordinary code, not by the AI.

How it learns, for you only

Chipi learns from what happens to your pitches: which channels get replies, which offers land, which edits you make to drafts. It uses that to improve your next search and your next draft.

  • It waits for evidence. A single reply doesn't change your strategy. Chipi suggests a change only once there's enough data to be fairly sure.
  • Big changes need your OK. Raising your rate or dropping a channel is proposed to you with the evidence, not applied silently.
  • Your data stays yours. What Chipi learns about you is used for you, not to train AI models.

Where it can be wrong

AI is useful and sometimes wrong. Please keep these in mind:

  • Rates are estimates. They come from published averages; your real rate depends on your client, your niche and how you negotiate.
  • Job listings change. A lead may be filled or changed after we found it. Always check the original post.
  • Reading a CV isn't perfect. Check your profile after uploading and correct anything that's off.
  • Drafts are starting points. You know your work and your client best; edit freely.
  • Tax and legal questions need a qualified adviser. Chipi's guidance on Dutch rules is general information, not advice.

The models we use

Chipi uses large language models from OpenAI through its business API. A larger model handles judgment (your offer, rate and drafts); smaller, faster models handle bulk work like ranking listings. By default, OpenAI doesn't use data sent through its API to train its models, and keeps requests for up to 30 days to detect abuse. More on data handling is in our privacy policy.

Rules we follow

  • EU AI Act: we tell you when you're interacting with an AI system, as the transparency rules in force since 2 August 2026 require.
  • GDPR: no fully automated decisions with significant effects about you (art. 22), and your rights over your data apply in full.
  • Dutch freelance rules: offers are written to describe genuinely independent work, with regard to the Wet DBA, which the Belastingdienst has enforced again since 1 January 2025.

Report a mistake

If Chipi got something wrong (a fact about you, a rate, an unfair suggestion), tell us at [hello@yourdomain.nl]. We read every report and use it to fix how Chipi works, not just the one result.
